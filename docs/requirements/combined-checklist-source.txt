Combined Legal Platform Master Checklist
Clio and LEAP requirements with the Reglify business planPrepared 31 October 2026
One platform for running a law firm, handling legal matters and recording compliance decisions. This checklist combines the two uploaded documents into one tracker with 192 checks across 24 sections. Shared features appear once.
What each source adds
Clio requirements in prompts.docx: dashboard, contacts, matters, time recording, billing, payments and financial reports.
LEAP requirements in prompts.docx: matter workspaces, document templates, workflows, trust accounting and Office integrations.
Reglify Business Plan v2.docx: client due diligence, independent risk rating, challenges, human reasoning, sealed evidence and compliance oversight.
These are source requirements and product ideas, not a verified catalogue of every current vendor feature. No source code, live services or prototype test results have been validated for this checklist.
How to track your work
Tick Done after the feature works and its completion check passes. The boxes can be ticked in Word or on a printed copy.
Write Left, In progress, Blocked, Later or N/A in Status. Later remains unfinished; N/A means an approved exclusion.
Keep proof by item ID, such as a test result, screenshot or short recording. All statuses start blank.
Core or launch describes the proposed role of a section, not an agreed deadline. Later modules need separate release approval.

How the three parts become one platform
A contact becomes a client and is linked to a matter. The same matter holds legal work, tasks, documents, time, invoices and compliance status. The compliance module assesses the recorded facts, challenges weak conclusions and records the solicitor's signed decision. Approved clearance is then visible wherever the matter is used.
Shared records prevent duplicate entry. Restricted SAR information still needs tighter access than ordinary matter data. A person able to view a matter must not automatically see every suspicion report.
Architecture decisions to record
Use one modular application with shared firm, user, contact and matter IDs. The backend enforces permissions and clearance rules.
The original prompt names PostgreSQL with Prisma; Reglify describes a document oriented store. This combined plan proposes PostgreSQL with versioned structured assessment data. Record the final design before building.
Use one private document store. The compliance module binds decisions to exact evidence versions or approved external references, rather than collecting a second copy of every identity document.
The internal practice and compliance modules communicate directly. Connecting to external Clio or LEAP is optional for import, migration or coexistence.
Keep ordinary audit logs and sealed compliance evidence distinct. Sealing makes specified changes detectable; it does not make a wrong decision correct.
Source conflicts and scope decisions
Pricing needs approval. Reglify lists Solo at £69 in its product table and £49 in its forecasts. It also describes both included AI drafting and a later AI add on. Its Small Firm top up figure needs arithmetic review. None of these prices is adopted as the price for your combined platform.
Legal rules need review. Regulatory citations, mandatory risk floors, reporting clocks and CQS requirements in the plan are proposed content for professional validation. This checklist does not certify their legal accuracy or currency.
Delivery timing needs a new estimate. Reglify's six month build covers its core compliance product. It does not estimate the complete practice, accounting and compliance platform. Its SAR and CQS modules are scheduled after the core launch; corporate checks and adjacent regulatory modules are later work.
AI drafting is included as a requirement with a full manual option. RAG is a separate scope decision. No extra RAG subsystem is assumed here.
Suggested delivery order
1. Shared foundation, firm isolation, contacts and matters.2. Core matter work, documents, time and basic billing.3. Compliance intake, approved rules, challenge flow and sealed decisions.4. Trust accounting, oversight and complete workflow verification.5. Security review, restore test, pilot and launch.6. Agreed SAR, CQS, corporate checks and external integrations.
